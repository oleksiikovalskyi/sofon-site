## 5. Extended paragraph (~700–900 chars)

V1) Cappers are usually blamed for capping problems. More often the fault is upstream of the head: caps arriving mis-oriented, jammed in the chute, or presented inconsistently to the chuck.

We engineer the whole cap handling path as change parts — cap chucks and inserts, cap sorter stars, cap distribution stars and guides, escapement blocks, cap chutes and cap tracks, turnover chutes and chute guarding. Each part is designed around the specific closure geometry rather than a nominal diameter, which matters as soon as a cap is oblong, tapered, asymmetric or has a hinged or pump feature.

Manufacturing capability and long experience with capping machinery across makes and models mean parts fit the machine you own. As new containers and closures are introduced, having an experienced partner familiar with the machinery is what keeps the introduction from becoming a project.  [879 chars]

V2) Cap handling covers everything between the cap hopper and the container thread, and every stage of it is a fit problem.

Sorter stars have to pick up caps reliably, including closures that don't sit parallel. Distribution stars and guides have to hold that orientation. Escapement blocks have to release one cap at a time, consistently. Chutes and turnover chutes have to move caps without lifting or jamming. Chucks have to grip and release without wearing the closure or themselves. Get one wrong and the capper's rated speed becomes theoretical.

We engineer all of it as change parts for your closures and your machine, in materials and finishes suited to your production environment, with the same colour-coding and identification approach used across the rest of the changeover set. Spare parts are available for the wear items that inevitably need them.  [860 chars]

## 8. Industry proof points

- **Beverage.** High-speed capping magnifies any inconsistency in cap presentation; parts engineered to the closure hold orientation through the transfer.
- **Food.** Wide closure ranges across SKUs mean the cap side of the changeover needs the same colour-coded discipline as the container side.
- **Pharmaceutical.** Small closures and regulated environments call for parts finished so that nothing collects, and sized for consistent single-cap escapement.
- **Personal care.** Pumps, triggers and decorative closures rarely sit parallel — sorter stars and chutes are engineered specifically for that.
- **Household chemicals.** Trigger sprays and child-resistant closures each bring their own presentation problem; parts are built per closure, not per category.
- **Automotive and lubricants.** Large, heavy closures on rigid containers wear chuck components; spare wear parts keep that predictable.
- **Contract packaging.** New closures arrive with every new customer. An existing catalogue of designs shortens the introduction considerably.

## 9. Booth answers

**"Our new cap keeps jamming in the chute."**
Then the chute geometry doesn't match the closure. That's a change part, not a machine problem — and adjustable cap chutes let you tune it in three axes afterwards.

**"Our caps aren't round."**
Oblong, tapered, asymmetric and non-parallel closures are routine work. The part is designed to the actual geometry.

**"Can you match our capper?"**
Yes. Cap handling parts are engineered for capping machinery across makes and models, to the mounting references already on the machine.

**"We spend hours clearing surplus caps."**
Cap clean-out modules purge caps from hoppers, elevators and chutes automatically. Manual clean-out is one of the bigger hidden causes of downtime on a capping line.

**"Do you supply the chucks as well as the parts around them?"**
Yes — chucks, inserts and the whole sorting and distribution path. Engineering it as one set is what makes it consistent.

**"What about torque control?"**
That's the headset rather than the change parts. Magnetic hysteresis headsets give non-contact, consistent torque, and we supply those alongside the cap handling parts.
