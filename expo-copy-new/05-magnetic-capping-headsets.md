## 5. Extended paragraph (~700–900 chars)

V1) Every capping problem eventually becomes a torque problem. Loose caps leak, tight caps fail consumer opening tests, and inconsistent caps do both in the same batch.

Magnetic hysteresis headsets solve it by removing contact from the torque path. Permanent magnets transmit torque across an air gap, so the value applied is set by the magnetic design rather than by the current condition of a mechanical clutch. Torque ranges from 0.6 to 5.6 N·m with simple, accurate adjustment, and stays where it was set.

The build supports that: stainless steel construction, fully sealed internals, and nickel-plated double-encapsulated magnets that make the headset impervious to caustic washdown. A patented adjustable top load reduces wear, and there are no components to service.

Practically: repeatable setups, better cap chuck wear than ratcheting heads, and 7,000-plus hours between rebuilds.  [888 chars]

V2) Ratcheting capping heads wear, and as they wear the torque they deliver drifts. That drift is invisible until it shows up as leakers or as failed opening-force tests, and correcting it means stopping to re-set heads that were correct at the start of the run.

Magnetic hysteresis headsets don't have that failure mode. Torque is transmitted magnetically, without contact, so it is consistent from the first cap to the last and repeatable across setups. The range covers 0.6 to 5.6 N·m and adjustment is straightforward.

Because there is no rubbing contact, cap chucks last longer too — a secondary saving that adds up across a fleet of heads. Run time between rebuilds exceeds 7,000 hours, sealed magnet assemblies carry a lifetime warranty, and refurbishment is available both in-house and in the field, restoring headsets to like-new condition and extending equipment life instead of replacing it.  [900 chars]

## 8. Industry proof points

- **Beverage.** Consistent closure torque across a high-speed capper is a quality parameter; non-contact torque holds it without mid-run head re-setting.
- **Food.** Caustic washdown routines are where sealed, stainless construction earns its cost — nothing to ingress, nothing to service.
- **Pharmaceutical.** Repeatable, documented torque matters in regulated packaging; magnetic transmission removes wear-driven drift from the equation.
- **Personal care.** Decorative and pump closures fail visibly when over-tightened; controlled torque protects both the seal and the appearance.
- **Household chemicals.** Child-resistant and trigger closures depend on torque accuracy for both safety and function.
- **Automotive and lubricants.** Heavy closures and long runs favour headsets that go 7,000-plus hours between rebuilds rather than heads that need routine attention.
- **Contract packaging.** Retrofittable, interchangeable headsets let one fleet of cappers serve customers with very different torque specifications.

## 9. Booth answers

**"We already have ratcheting heads."**
Then you already have torque drift; it just hasn't been measured yet. Magnetic headsets are interchangeable with heads on a wide range of capping machinery, so this is usually a retrofit rather than a machine change.

**"What torque range?"**
0.6 to 5.6 N·m, adjusted simply and accurately, and it stays where you set it because nothing in the torque path wears.

**"How often do they need rebuilding?"**
7,000-plus hours of run time between rebuilds, and the sealed magnet assemblies carry a lifetime warranty.

**"We do caustic washdown."**
Stainless steel construction, fully sealed internals and double-encapsulated magnets. Washdown is the design case, not the exception.

**"What happens when one does need service?"**
Refurbishment is available both in-house and in the field, restoring the headset to like-new condition rather than replacing it.

**"Will it wear our cap chucks?"**
Less than a ratcheting head does. Removing mechanical contact from the torque path is what improves chuck life.
