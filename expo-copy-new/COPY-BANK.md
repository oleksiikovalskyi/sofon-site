# Trade show copy bank — brief and ground rules

**What this is.** Ready-to-place English copy for exhibition handouts and stand
graphics, drawn from an existing body of product and application content on
packaging line changeover and container handling. Text only — no image
selection, no layout.

**How to use it.** Every direction is a *bank of blocks*, not a finished text.
Each block gives several interchangeable variants. The designer picks one variant
per block and assembles them to fit the layout. Blocks are ordered from largest
type size to smallest.

## Block structure (same in every file)

| Block | What it is | Target length |
|---|---|---|
| 1. Headlines | Main line of the panel or handout | 20–60 chars |
| 2. Subheads | Second line under the headline | 50–110 chars |
| 3. Short paragraph | Caption under an image, sidebar | ~150 chars |
| 4. Standard paragraph | Main body of a section | ~300–350 chars |
| 5. Extended paragraph | Full page or back-of-leaflet copy | ~700–900 chars |
| 6. Benefit bullets | Bulleted list, parallel phrasing | 25–60 chars each |
| 7. Technical facts | Numbers, materials, applications, coverage | varies |
| 8. Industry proof points | One line per industry | 90–160 chars each |
| 9. Booth answers | Replies to the questions visitors actually ask | 1–3 sentences |
| 10. CTAs | Buttons, QR captions, closing lines | 15–45 chars |
| 11. Micro-copy | Badges, stickers, labels, icon captions | 8–30 chars |

Variants are numbered `V1)`, `V2)` … with the exact character count in brackets
at the end of the line, so text can be fitted to a layout without recounting.

## Ground rules applied throughout

- **English only.**
- **No trade marks and no product brand names.** Registered product names from
  the source material are replaced by plain descriptive equivalents —
  "quick-adjust guide rails", "changeover cart", "automated guide rail
  adjustment". No registered or trade mark symbol appears anywhere in the bank.
- **No machine manufacturer names.** The source material names dozens of OEMs in
  gallery captions and retrofit lists. None are carried over. Machines are
  described by function — filler, capper, labeller, rinser, monoblock,
  unscrambler, case packer, blow moulder.
- **No customer names.** Examples appear anonymized by industry —
  "a household cleaning products manufacturer", "a personal care plant", never
  the company.
- **No personal data.** No individual names, job titles tied to a named employer,
  email addresses or phone numbers. Testimonials in the source are used only as
  the underlying fact, never as an attributed quote.
- **No geography.** No countries, cities, regions, plant locations, customs or
  trade-agreement references, and no country-specific regulator names.
  Regulatory language is written generically — "food-contact compliant",
  "cleanroom compliant", "regulated production environments".
- **No company-scale figures.** Founding year, years in business, headcount,
  number of countries served, number of machine models covered and similar
  corporate metrics are deliberately left out. Where the source used them as
  proof, the copy uses a capability statement instead — "nearly every machine
  make and model" rather than a count.
- **Facts come from the source material only.** No invented speeds, materials or
  claims. Performance results quoted in the source (OEE gains, changeover time
  reductions, adjustment accuracy) are retained, phrased as results reported by
  users rather than as guarantees.
- **No commercial terms.** Prices, discounts and payback calculations tied to a
  specific customer's shift pattern are left out of print materials. ROI is
  stated only in the general form the source states it.
- **All units are metric.** Feet and inches converted to metres and millimetres,
  US gallons to litres, pound-inches of torque to newton-metres. Rounded
  sensibly for print — 24 m of guide rail, 50 mm of adjustment, 19 L containers,
  0.6–5.6 N·m of capping torque.
- **Throughput is stated in containers per hour.** The source material states
  speed in containers per minute; every figure here is that number × 60.
  Micro-copy badges use the short form, "120,000/hour".
- Wording is new marketing copy written from the source content — not a verbatim
  quote of it, so the same page can be reused online without duplicate text.
