## 8. Industry proof points

- **Beverage.** High-speed lines punish handling errors. Neck grippers and stars engineered for the container hold position at speed without scuffing or marking.
- **Food.** Multi-SKU plants change over several times a shift; standardised, colour-coded change part sets take the guesswork out of every one of them.
- **Pharmaceutical.** Parts can be built with hidden fasteners and finished surfaces so there is nowhere for product or residue to collect in a regulated environment.
- **Personal care.** Complex bottle and cap geometry is where generic parts fail. Simulated contact geometry protects premium finishes on the way through.
- **Household chemicals.** Material selection anticipates what the product does to the part — corrosion-resistant options for aggressive chemistry.
- **Automotive and lubricants.** Heavy, awkward containers wear parts fast. Exchangeable pocket inserts turn that wear into a planned swap.
- **Contract packaging.** An extensive catalogue of existing container designs means a new job often starts from a part that already exists.

## 9. Booth answers

**"We didn't buy the machine from you."**
That's the normal case. We engineer parts for nearly every machine make and model, and we cut them to the change part mounting references already in your machine.

**"We're adding a container size next quarter."**
Send us the container. We measure it and the machine digitally, model the handling in 3D and simulate it before manufacture, so the first set fits.

**"The machine spec says our new bottle won't run."**
Sometimes the spec is right and often it isn't. Skip-feed designs, offset pitch and custom star geometry have kept a lot of lines out of a capital purchase.

**"Our stars wear out too fast."**
Then don't replace the star. Exchangeable pocket inserts carry the contact profile in a premium material — you swap the insert, not the whole part.

**"Operators put the wrong parts on."**
Colour-coding and engraving stop that at the shelf, and puzzle-cut geometry with locating pins stops it at the machine.

**"How do you know it'll fit?"**
Because it's measured, modelled and simulated before it's machined — not fitted on your line at your expense.
