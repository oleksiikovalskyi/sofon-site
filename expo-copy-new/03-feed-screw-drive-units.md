## 8. Industry proof points

- **Beverage.** High-speed configurations reach 120,000 containers per hour, with synchronisation holding timing between machines instead of relying on accumulation.
- **Food.** Recipe-driven settings mean a changeover recalls known-good timing rather than an operator rediscovering it every run.
- **Pharmaceutical.** Vial-scale handling with fault detection and inspection integration suits regulated lines where a mis-timed container is a quality event.
- **Personal care.** Orienting, tilting and rotating applications put complex containers in a defined position before labelling, filling or capping.
- **Household chemicals.** Washdown-capable construction and low-maintenance design stand up to caustic cleaning routines.
- **Automotive and lubricants.** Separating and metering large oil containers is a drive problem as much as a screw problem — dual synchronised drives handle the mass.
- **Pet care.** Large-format containers need precise spacing before filling; programmable timing keeps that consistent across SKUs.

## 9. Booth answers

**"Will it talk to our controls?"**
Yes. The unit integrates into your existing control system, or runs stand-alone with its own if that's simpler.

**"We run everything from vials to big jugs."**
That's the range the units are built for — pharmaceutical vials up to 19-litre containers. The drive is sized to the application.

**"How fast can it go?"**
High-speed applications run up to 120,000 containers per hour. The limit is usually the container, not the drive.

**"Do we need a person to retune it after a changeover?"**
Recipes hold the settings per SKU. The operator selects, the unit recalls, and the screw comes off and on without tools.

**"Servo or AC?"**
Depends on what the application needs from the timing. We build both, and we'll tell you which one your job actually justifies.

**"Can you supply the screws too?"**
Yes — and it's usually better if we do. Screw and drive designed together behave better than screw and drive introduced to each other on your line.
