## 5. Extended paragraph (~700–900 chars)

V1) Timing screws — also called feed screws, worms, augers, scrolls and infeed screws — are the component that decides whether a packaging line runs or fights itself. They feed the machine, and along the way they set orientation, pitch and spacing.

We are a leading supplier of timing screws to machine builders, and the range of what a single screw can do is wider than most plants use: feed, meter, turn, group, collate, index, dwell, invert, combine, divide, dead plate transfer, orient and denest.

Screws are manufactured in UHMW, nylon, acetal, HDPE and stainless steel, with UHMW available in a range of colours to speed changeover identification. A square drive means the screw can only be fitted one way, tool-lessly. A urethane driver option shows wear visually, so replacement is a decision rather than a discovery.  [823 chars]

V2) The most expensive timing screw is the worn one still running. As the plastic rounds off and the leading edges flatten, containers start to scuff, spacing drifts, and downstream machines begin rejecting product that was fine when it entered the line.

Replacement is straightforward. If we made the original screw, a reference number is enough to have a new one manufactured. If it didn't, we reproduce it anyway — down to the identical steelwork — by taking measurements of the existing screw and its mounting.

The same route covers new containers. Send the specifications of the container you're adding and we design a screw that fits the machine you already own, so a new SKU doesn't turn into a capital project. Every screw and all steelwork is manufactured in our own plant.  [780 chars]

## 9. Booth answers

**"Ours came with the machine — can you match them?"**
Yes. We reproduce screws from any manufacturer, down to the identical steelwork. If you can't find a reference number, we'll take the measurements.

**"How do we know when to replace?"**
Discoloured or gouged plastic, rounded thread edges, flattened threads, worn coupling pins. If containers have started scuffing, the screw is already past due. A urethane driver makes wear visible before it reaches the product.

**"We're changing the container next year."**
Then talk to us before the container is finalized. A design consultation at that stage often saves a machine modification later — pitch, length and diameter are easier to solve on paper.

**"Can a screw really do all that?"**
One screw feeds, meters, turns, groups, indexes, inverts, combines, divides and orients. Most lines use a fraction of what the component can do.

**"What's the lead time?"**
We manufacture all steelwork and screws in our own plant, so the schedule is ours to control rather than a supplier's.
