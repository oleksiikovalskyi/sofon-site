## 5. Extended paragraph (~700–900 chars)

V1) We are a manufacturing company. Timing screws, change parts, drive units and custom container handling machines are all made in our own plant, and that same capacity is available to customers who arrive with their own drawings.

Manufacture to print means what it says: you provide the prints, we produce the component. What you're buying alongside the machining is a half-century of understanding what these parts do — every one of them touches a container, and the finish at those contact points determines whether the container arrives on the shelf looking the way you intended.

Materials cover UHMW, nylon, acetal, HDPE and stainless steel, with UHMW available in a range of colours. And because the steelwork is made in the same building, there's no second supplier in the schedule.  [788 chars]

V2) Not every part arrives with a drawing. Where the only reference is the component already running in the machine, our 3D measuring technology models the existing setup precisely — we reproduce parts down to identical steelwork, regardless of who originally manufactured them, and confirm the new part fits the core plates already installed.

That capability is what turns a discontinued part into a solved problem, and it's why replacement work doesn't require the original supplier's cooperation.

The practical benefit is lead time. Manufacturing in house means ordering from the people who machine the part rather than waiting out someone else's queue, and for change parts already in our system a replacement can be in your hands within 24 hours.  [749 chars]

## 8. Industry proof points

- **Alcohol.** Wine and spirits lines run corkers, capsulators, foilers and monoblocks — machines whose contact parts are almost never available quickly from the original source.
- **Beverage.** When supplied parts arrived with fitment problems, on-site measurement, design correction, manufacture and international shipment were completed in seven business days.
- **Dairy.** Contact parts for cup, tub and carton lines machined in UHMW, where surface finish decides whether the pack reaches the shelf unmarked.
- **Food.** Colour-coded part sets machined per container size, so an operator identifies the right set without reading a drawing.
- **Household products.** Guides and stars for ovals, bricks and trigger sprays — geometries that rarely exist as a catalogue item.
- **Personal care.** Premium containers and delicate glass, where finishing at the contact point is the entire specification.
- **Automotive and petroleum.** Heavy 0.95 L and 3.8 L containers that wear guides quickly, making in-house replacement supply a scheduling question rather than a purchasing one.
